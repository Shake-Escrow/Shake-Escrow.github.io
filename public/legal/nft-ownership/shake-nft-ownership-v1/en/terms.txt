1. NFT Ownership and Transaction Settlement

1.1 Temporary Smart Contract Escrow
The NFT represents ownership of the physical asset identified in this Agreement, subject to the applicable transaction, settlement, and return provisions. Temporary holding of the NFT by a designated escrow smart contract serves solely to secure and execute an authorized transaction. Such holding does not, by itself, transfer ownership of the physical asset to the smart contract, Shake Defi, Inc., or the designated Arbiter.

The smart contract shall transfer or return the NFT according to the selected transaction mechanism and its applicable authorization requirements: two matching participant authorizations for 2-of-3 escrow; the applicable release or refund conditions for time-based escrow; or immediate exchange upon successful payment for a zero-day transaction. Any applicable physical-goods return requirements remain conditions of refund settlement.

Transfer into escrow alone does not constitute a completed sale. Ownership changes between the transaction parties according to this Agreement and the applicable confirmed settlement. Physical delivery, return obligations, and risk of loss remain governed by the applicable transaction terms.

2. Parties, Scope and Identification

2.1 Who Accepts These Terms
These Terms govern the NFT ownership registration or transfer identified in the record presented to you. The person registering an item is the Registrant; the person transferring ownership is the Seller or Transferor; the person acquiring ownership is the Buyer or Recipient. Merchant and Client retain their applicable meanings in the Shake Terms of Service. Each accepting person agrees with Shake Defi, Inc. (the Company) concerning use of its ownership-record services. In a transaction, the Seller and Buyer also make the transfer, payment and delivery promises stated here and in the exact transaction terms directly to each other. Each is a party to those transaction promises, rather than an unidentified beneficiary of another person's agreement. An Arbiter does not become the physical owner by participating.

2.2 The Item and Its NFT
The Item is the particular physical asset identified by its permanent numeric reference, three identification keywords and original ownership document. The corresponding NFT is identified by its blockchain network, collection and token identifier, together with its NFT item-contract address where applicable. Each sale has a separate transaction reference. Similar words, descriptions, photographs or tokens on another network do not establish the same identity. Before minting, the declaration identifies the preallocated item reference, network, collection and intended owner wallet; the confirmed token identifier is linked after issuance.

2.3 Separate Ownership Facts
The original declarant, current blockchain token holder, person claiming legal ownership, person who has accepted these Terms, and person physically possessing the Item may differ. Blockchain records show token movements; they do not independently prove lawful physical title, actual delivery or acceptance by a person. Ownership labels in the App must be read with the evidence and limitations displayed.

3. Registration and the Original Declaration

3.1 Ownership and Authority
The Registrant declares that they own the identified Item and have authority to register its ownership NFT. A person acting for a legal owner must identify that owner and their representative capacity and have the necessary authority; the App may support only the registration capacities it can properly record. Joint ownership or restricted authority must not be represented as unrestricted sole ownership.

3.2 Item Information and Restrictions
The Registrant must provide accurate, non-misleading identifying information and disclose known liens, joint ownership, competing claims, existing ownership NFTs or other restrictions affecting transfer authority. A Seller must disclose material changes and restrictions in the sale record before acceptance. If the App cannot record or accommodate a restriction or representative capacity, the person must not proceed as though it were absent. A disclosure does not remove a restriction or obtain a third party's permission.

3.3 Declaration and Document Package
Before finger-signing, the Registrant is shown the full, exact ownership-terms version for the registration, with an opportunity to read and download it, and records an explicit acceptance. The signed PDF contains the concise declaration and identifying documents; it incorporates the full terms by their version, language, permanent public page and download URLs, and SHA-256 of the exact published terms snapshot. The full ownership terms are not reproduced as pages to finger-sign. The public snapshot must be available and must match the version actually presented and accepted.

For a user-supplied PDF, the uploaded pages are preserved as supplied and a concise standard declaration/reference addendum is clearly disclosed and presented before signing. The Registrant signs the uploaded pages and that addendum, after reviewing and accepting the full public terms separately. An uploaded document does not bypass the standard declaration, and conflicting material terms must be resolved before registration proceeds. Signing captures private evidence only under the separately presented signing permissions; accepting the terms alone captures no signing photograph.

3.4 Original Document and Registration Limits
The final signed ownership document is preserved as exact bytes, with its SHA-256 recorded through issuance. The original declaration is a dated record of its signer's statements; it is not a declaration by later owners. Subsequent acceptances and supplemental records are separately dated and linked. Minting, profile information, signatures and photographs do not constitute an independent guarantee by the Company of title, authenticity, condition or possession.

4. Ownership Representation and Transfers

4.1 Agreed Ownership Representation
For the identified Item, accepting parties adopt its corresponding NFT as the digital representation and record of physical ownership under these Terms, the exact transaction terms and applicable law. The Company does not own the Item or grant a revocable license to own it. These Terms do not override mandatory law, establish that the transferor had valid title, or extinguish rights of persons who have not agreed.

4.2 Seller and Transferor Obligations
When transferring ownership of the Item, an accepting Seller or Transferor agrees to transfer the corresponding NFT to the intended Recipient, arrange physical delivery as agreed, disclose material transfer restrictions and provide the Recipient the applicable ownership and transaction terms for acceptance. The Seller or Transferor must obtain the Recipient's acceptance for an intended ownership transfer. They must not knowingly present the Item and corresponding NFT as independently owned assets or create a competing ownership NFT for the same Item. Temporary technical holding and a move between wallets of the same owner are addressed separately below.

4.3 Buyer and Recipient Obligations
A Buyer or Recipient accepts the relationship between the identified Item and corresponding NFT and their own obligations under these Terms and the exact transaction terms. They must comply with applicable payment and return obligations and, when later transferring ownership, with the transfer obligations in Section 4.2. Each later Seller reaffirms their authority for the new transaction and each later Buyer makes their own acceptance. An original declaration, previous purchase or earlier acceptance does not replace acceptance of materially different terms for a later transaction.

4.4 Settlement, Physical Title and Delivery
As between accepting Seller and Buyer, the intended ownership transfer accompanies the lawful, authorized and confirmed completion of the selected payment/NFT exchange, subject to the exact transaction's expressly agreed title conditions and applicable law. A sale must specify physical delivery arrangements, applicable risk of loss, material restrictions and any additional title conditions before authorization. Token delivery or payment confirmation alone does not certify physical receipt or defeat another person's lawful claim. Taking possession does not itself prove payment or NFT delivery. Submission, timeout or partial blockchain execution must not be represented as completed exchange.

4.5 The Three Transaction Mechanisms
For 2-of-3 escrow, settlement requires two matching valid authorizations from the Buyer, Seller and designated Arbiter; no single participant can settle alone and no automatic timed release is implied. For time-based escrow, the applicable release eligibility, dispute extensions, early collection and refund rules remain in force; expiration alone is not execution confirmation. For no escrow, there is no customer holding period and no sale PDF or finger-signing step: exact transaction acceptance and wallet authorization are required. Base exchange must confirm the required payment/NFT exchange together; TON may require multiple messages and is complete only when all required legs are verified. Existing escrow sale-PDF signing is separate from ownership-term modal acceptance and remains required unless an expressly approved transaction policy replaces it.

4.6 Returns and Refunds
Existing applicable return and refund provisions are preserved. Company-authorized Involuntary Refunds governed by ToS Sections 5.3–5.5 retain the accepted proof-of-return-shipment condition; these Terms do not replace it with a requirement that the Seller first confirm receipt. Voluntary Refunds under ToS Section 4.5 and independent 2-of-3 outcomes under ToS Section 5.9 retain their distinct rules. NFT disposition must correspond to the authorized return/refund outcome. After completed transfer, a subsequent refund involving a return of the NFT requires a new authorized exchange with the person entitled to transfer it; an earlier Seller's signature does not authorize taking it from a later holder.

5. Wallet Moves and Transfers Outside Shake

5.1 Own-Wallet Moves and Temporary Holding
A move between wallets controlled by the same owner does not by itself create a new person-to-person ownership transfer. The owner must verify authority over the new wallet before new authorized actions through Shake. Designated escrow holding remains temporary custody as stated in Section 1.1. Neither a sender's label nor a token event conclusively establishes that two wallets have the same owner.

5.2 Gifts and External Transfers
NFTs may be transferred to arbitrary recipient wallets without the recipient using Shake. An accepting Transferor's promise to obtain acceptance remains their obligation; token receipt does not establish that the Recipient accepted. Shake may record an external token transfer while recording acceptance, sale terms and physical delivery as unknown. A Recipient who later uses Shake may explicitly accept the relevant item and transaction terms at that time. Such acceptance is not backdated and does not retrospectively validate earlier title or delivery.

5.3 Theft, Lost Keys and Other Exceptions
An unauthorized token movement does not by itself establish lawful transfer of the Item. These Terms do not create a power for Shake to confiscate an Item, force an NFT transfer, replace a lost token or cancel legal ownership. Theft, lost keys, destruction, inheritance and court-directed transfers remain subject to applicable law and any separately supported procedures. Reports of a problem are claims with provenance, not conclusive findings of title.

6. Intentional Acceptance and Records

6.1 Ownership-Term Acceptance
Buyers and Sellers may accept these ownership terms by an intentional affirmative action in a modal that identifies their role, the Item, exact NFT and transaction, and gives clear access to the full terms version. The acceptance control is not preselected. Opening a page, scrolling, joining a role, connecting a wallet or merely receiving a token does not constitute this recorded acceptance. No drawn signature or camera photo is required by this ownership-term modal; any separately required escrow document signing is a different action.

6.2 Exact Terms and Wallet Authorization
Acceptance records identify the actual version and language presented, participant, wallet relationship, context and server-recorded time. Exact transaction terms include the parties, NFT, payment asset and amount, mechanism, delivery and other material terms. Required wallet authorization is a separate step bound to the applicable exact terms. Changing a material term requires renewed acceptance and authorization from affected parties. Accepting a pending transaction does not state that purchase, physical receipt or settlement has occurred.

6.3 Records and Readable Copies
Participants are provided access to a retainable copy of their accepted text and receipt under the applicable access policy. Legal-version snapshots and finalized ownership documents are preserved without replacement by a mutable latest page. Transaction parties may access relevant acceptance evidence for their transaction under the Privacy Policy; private authentication credentials and signing evidence are not published. Canceling a pending transaction may end its eligibility for execution but does not erase the fact of a prior acceptance.

7.1 Separate Evidence Permissions
Ownership-term acceptance does not provide consent to front-camera capture, long-term retention of signing photos or buyer-audit disclosure. Those choices are governed by the separate NFT signing permissions and Privacy Policy. A later owner acquires no unrestricted right to earlier signers' private photographs or identity evidence. Any buyer audit remains subject to the signer's permitted purposes and a named, scoped, time-limited grant.

7.2 Public and Private Records
The original signed ownership PDF and designated item photographs are public provenance artifacts. Participants must review the public content before signing, including the name in the declaration. Private evidence and detailed acceptance records remain subject to access controls. The Company cannot promise to remove public blockchain commitments or copies already obtained by others. Acceptance record retention and requests concerning personal data are governed by the applicable Privacy Policy and legal retention obligations.

8. Versions, Existing Records and Applicable Terms

8.1 Original and Existing Records
The original declaration retains the version accepted when it was signed. A later person's acceptance does not change its bytes or its signer's historical promises. Existing NFTs without recorded acceptance of a later version remain labeled accordingly; use of the new sale pathway may require fresh acceptance before a new commitment. No acceptance is inferred or backfilled solely from earlier registration or token holding.

Publishing a new version does not insert it into an existing signed PDF or establish that its signer accepted it. Any later acceptance or supplemental declaration is a separate, dated record. A finalized but unminted document is not silently replaced: any changed declaration or references must be reviewed and signed as a new package, or handled through an explicitly supported supplemental acceptance path. A submitted mint must be reconciled before any retry.

8.2 Changes and In-Flight Transactions
New versions apply only as stated in their published effective terms and actual acceptance records. They do not silently rewrite an earlier participant's promises or third-party rights. Material changes for a new transaction require fresh acceptance. A funded or committed transaction must retain access to its accepted terms and applicable release, refund and recovery process; accepting a later version must not become a retroactive condition for recovering committed assets.

8.3 Coordination with ToS and Transaction Terms
Mandatory law applies first. For the identified transaction, these separately accepted ownership terms govern the NFT/physical-item relationship and exact agreed transaction terms govern price, payment asset, delivery and the selected mechanism, consistently with applicable settlement, fee, dispute and return provisions of the ToS. Changes to those baseline provisions require explicit coordinated agreement; a conflicting uploaded document or label must not silently bypass them. Transferring an NFT does not assign a Shake account or delegate previously incurred obligations. The versioned ownership terms, transaction terms and applicable ToS/Privacy versions must be identified before acceptance.

8.4 Continuing Duties and Remedies
An owner's duty to perform an accepted transfer, deliver the Item, satisfy an applicable return obligation or answer for earlier representations does not disappear merely because they transfer the token or stop using Shake. Remedies and dispute procedures remain subject to the applicable accepted agreement and law. No unaccepted third party is deemed bound by another person's declaration; no new arbitration agreement or waiver is imposed solely through a token event.
